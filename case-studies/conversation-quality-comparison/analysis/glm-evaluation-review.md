# GLM Evaluation Review

Independent review of GLM5.3 evaluations | CVAS model trials | 15 October 2026

## Overall assessment

GLM is useful as a first-pass evaluator, but these trials do not support relying on it as the sole final grader. It catches important factual errors, applies much of the answer key correctly and produces traceable explanations. Its recurring weakness is excessive confidence in the reports and in its own verification: it misses consequential overclaims, gives some incomplete reasoning full credit, and occasionally introduces factual errors. [GL; GH; GQ]

The most consistent positive result is its recognition of the shared contractual gaps. In all three revision 1 evaluations it identifies the missing C016 incorporated-standard consequence and the designated-contact uncertainty. It also catches Luna's incorrect selection-route label, Haiku's invented C035 defect and Qwen's false queue statuses and assumed booking. Those findings materially improve the original reports.

The strongest reservation concerns summary judgment. Phrases such as excellent factual discipline, every trap avoided and errors confined to contract are broader than the evidence supports. The problem is not that every missed detail should receive a large penalty. It is that some missed claims affect required conclusions about population inference, evidence scope and feasible implementation.

## Scope and interpretation

The principal comparison covers three supplied GLM evaluations under answer-key calibration revision 1: Luna's rerun, Haiku and Qwen. The original Luna evaluation is considered separately as calibration history. The reports and source records were checked during the trial reviews; this assessment identifies confirmed discrepancies and reasoned grading disagreements, rather than calculating an exhaustive reviewer error rate.

Independent reviewer describes GLM's intended role. The files do not independently establish its execution context, prior exposure or separation from the report-producing sessions. No claim of procedural independence or reviewer bias follows from the label alone. The conclusions concern the supplied evaluation outputs.

## Where GLM added value

### Correct findings across the trials

- Luna: catches C016 being labeled routine rather than member_or_family_concern, identifies the missing 72-member/repeated-observation limitation, and recognizes the two required contractual omissions. [GL]
- Haiku: catches the invented C035 defect, C052's erased initial lapse, C007's wrong cue label, the missing training-use analysis and the overreach from a packet summary to actual reporting deficiencies. [GH]
- Qwen: catches six forwarded items being reported as seven, incorrect statuses for PD-26-0005/0009/0046, the unsupported already-booked contact, and the shared contract omissions. [GQ]
- After calibration: uses qualitative judgments, gives grouped coverage credit, avoids treating an appendix as mandatory and applies the already-expired-deadline rule. These are real improvements in its evaluations. [K; GL; GH; GQ]

## Grading judgments needing more care

### Future change control

GLM gives full feasibility credit to both Luna and Haiku. Luna requires evidence of correction before the vote without explicitly preserving the notice minimum for new material changes. Haiku requests an interim rollback or suspension that CVAS can approve quickly under sections 6.1/6.2. Quick approval does not replace the ten-Business-Day notice requirement. [GL; GH; A, section 6; K]

Both reports have defensible renewal fallbacks, and Haiku phrases the interim action as a request. Neither should be accused of a proven contractual breach or an unambiguously impossible deployment mandate. The narrower judgment is that the implementation path is underexplained and merits partial credit. This is a grading disagreement with GLM, not a factual error equivalent to misreading feedback. Qwen's explicit notice-and-approval path is better supported. [L, conditions; H, page 12; Q, section 9.2]

### Caveats do not cancel contradictory claims

Haiku warns against release attribution but also says the change did not produce improvement in the sample. Its M-011 narrative describes isolation growing, although the earlier returning-member records show chosen solitude and boundaries. Those statements require qualification. GLM's broad claim that every trap is avoided overlooks them. Acknowledging these issues should not erase Haiku's otherwise useful sampling cautions or imply that it explicitly recommended social pressure. [GH; H, pages 3 and 6; C]

Qwen's limitations section is detailed, yet its opening says the service works for most members most of the time and its conclusion calls failures narrow and recent. The report also says later continuity makes C005's service outcome visible despite the truncated exchange. Those statements are not made sound by an accurate disclaimer elsewhere. GLM gives the relevant evidence and sampling conclusions too much credit. [GQ; Q, lines 12, 162, 196 and 237]

### Error consequences and aggregation

Haiku's invented C035 defect is one underlying error, but it appears in several sections and affects its relevance-failure comparison between releases. Its reach is wider than a single minor row, even though it does not change the separate Problem-or-Critical percentages. Qwen's queue errors likewise supply false status evidence for an unresolved-ticket list and remediation condition. These uses should inform consequence without multiplying penalties for repeated wording. [GH; GQ]

Grouping Qwen's status errors into one error class may be useful for reporting, but a common underlying cause has not been established. The key prevents duplicate deductions for one error; it does not require every independently wrong fact in a category to be treated as the same error. Neither automatic accumulation nor automatic consolidation is justified. [K]

## Consistency and recommended use

### Where summaries exceed the detailed judgments

In Qwen's evaluation, GLM marks the undocumented routine-selection conclusion partially met, then says all sampling conclusions are met in the final summary. It also says errors are confined to contract while missing opening population generalizations. A final verdict should preserve limitations already found in the scorecard and account for contradictory claims elsewhere. [GQ]

For Haiku, GLM says the three supported vendor claims are never credited, including timely notices. Haiku explicitly credits all six timely notices and uses the 1,367-session denominator. Missing availability reconciliation and incomplete mixed-claim synthesis can justify partial credit; the categorical explanation is too broad. [GH; H, sections 4 and 8]

### Calibration history

The initial Luna evaluation correctly caught the invented C038 defect. It placed too much weight on the absence of individual case citations, although the key already permitted grouping, and gave an A-minus/roughly 90% assessment without a defined numerical rubric. That assessment predates revision 1's explicit qualitative-only instruction, so it is a calibration weakness rather than a violation of a later rule. The later evaluations show improved adherence to the revised format. [GI; K]

### A practical reviewer protocol

- Record a finding with the exact report passage, source location, source fact and grading consequence. Label confirmed factual errors separately from interpretation or partial-credit disagreements.
- Check summaries against evidence. Reconcile executive claims with limitations, individual case ratings with totals, and final category labels with the scorecard. Do not let an accurate caveat automatically excuse an overclaim.
- Audit future actions independently of historical compliance. A past approved release does not establish a compliant schedule for a proposed rollback or correction. Test notice, approval, continuity and fallback conditions separately.
- Review the application of the rubric. Give equivalent grouped evidence equivalent credit; preserve valid alternative renewal decisions; count repeated errors once while considering their actual downstream use.
- Keep human source verification for final consequential judgments. Use GLM to organize checks and identify candidates for correction, then verify the passages supporting a model comparison or final grade.
These trials support using GLM as an assisted reviewer with a human verification step. They do not establish an overall error rate, a stable tendency across unrelated tasks, or the reviewer's relative performance against other graders. The answer key remains fixed for this completed comparison set.
