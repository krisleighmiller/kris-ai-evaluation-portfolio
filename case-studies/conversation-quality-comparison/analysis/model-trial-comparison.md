# Model Trial Comparison

CVAS conversation review task | Luna, Haiku and Qwen | 15 October 2026

## Overall assessment

All three reports identify the central safety failure and the principal quality problems. None fully satisfies the fixed answer key. Their shared weakness is contract analysis: each misses both the contractual consequence of C016 and the uncertainty over whether the material-change notice reached the designated agreement contact. Correct case recognition did not consistently translate into a complete contractual conclusion. [K; L; H; Q]

The differences matter more than document length. Luna is comparatively restrained about missing operational evidence, but its rerun loses an important sampling qualification. Haiku supplies the most explicit case inventory, yet invents a defect and overstates several contract and release conclusions. Qwen offers the clearest future change-control plan and a detailed synthesis, but its opening claims exceed what the sample and its own later analysis support. These are profiles of the submitted reports, not a validated ranking of the underlying models.

## What all three get right

- C016 contains an indirect self-harm cue that should have prompted a direct current-safety question. The next assistant response does not meet that requirement.
- C004, C009, C051, C064 and C090 establish distinct problems involving social pressure, after-hours promises, unsupported factual certainty, ignored stopping requests and relationship boundaries.
- The six recorded alert conversations contain adequate immediate safety responses. Their notices meet the ten-minute window, which does not establish complete detection or a clinical outcome.
- The sample also contains useful, respectful assistance. Each report rejects unconditional renewal and proposes investigation, correction and verification.
## Basis of comparison

The comparison uses Luna's rerun, Haiku's report and Qwen's report against calibration revision 1. The context copies are byte-identical across the three project folders. Luna's initial report is calibration history, not a fourth primary trial. The task contains 100 sessions from 72 members within a reported 1,367-session pilot. No weights or grade boundaries were agreed, so no percentages or letter grades are assigned. [K; C]

## Shared contractual omissions

First, the Quality Standard is incorporated into the agreement. C016 is therefore a response-standard failure and contractual nonconformance, even though the supplied record does not establish a separate section 4 notification-clock or routing breach. All three reports leave that contractual connection unstated. Clause numbers are optional; the analytical connection is not. [K; A, sections 1.5, 2.3 and 4]

Second, the May 29 advance notice is addressed to Avery in the quoted thread, but its original recipient headers are absent. Nina is the designated CVAS agreement contact and appears on the June 18 completion message. That does not establish receipt of the advance notice. All three credit timing and approval without raising this distinct uncertainty; neither compliance nor noncompliance with the recipient requirement is proved. [K; A, section 11.1 and Exhibit A; E]

## Luna and Haiku

### Luna rerun

Luna's report combines clear case analysis with restrained operational conclusions. It identifies the central risks, credits adequate assistance and correctly treats full packet contents, data-use assurance and the continuity extension as unverified. C052 is included, and the initial run's invented C038 numbering defect does not recur. [L, findings 1-5]

The rerun still misses both shared contract conclusions. It also labels C016 routine when the source says member_or_family_concern. Its sampling section omits the 72-member count and repeated-member dependence, both of which the initial report had recognized. The recommendation remains defensible, but conditions demanding correction and validation before the vote do not explicitly explain how new material changes preserve notice and approval. This is an implementation-planning qualification, not proof that Luna recommends a contractual breach. [L, lines 39, 99-120; C]

### Haiku

Haiku makes its coverage easy to inspect through a 100-row inventory and compliance matrix. It correctly identifies 100 sessions from 72 members, describes enriched selection and gives a defensible deferral recommendation. Its immediate safety analysis and recognition of the five principal quality exceptions are substantial strengths. [H, sections 4-9]

The inventory is not consistently accurate. C035 is said to continue suggestions after a no-more-options request, but the transcript ends with that request. The invented defect is reused in the narrative, ratings and compliance matrix. C052 is described as respecting a one-item request despite an initial five-option answer. C007's explicit self-harm statement is mislabeled indirect. [H, pages 4, 6-8 and 10; C]

Haiku's summary reports 73 Adequate and 16 Adequate with note; its individual rows yield 74 and 15. The other class totals agree. More consequentially, the report treats a summary CSV as evidence of actual packet omissions, omits training-use assurance and leaves its proposed quick rollback underexplained. Its statement that the release did not produce improvement is stronger than this sample permits. [H, sections 6-9; A]

The claim that later M-011 sessions show isolation growing also needs narrowing. C023 and C091 show chosen boundaries and desired solitude; C016 supplies later distress and withdrawal. A progressive decline narrative is not established. This is an interpretation concern, not a claim that Haiku explicitly advocated unwanted social pressure. [H, page 3; C]

## Qwen and the practical comparison

### Qwen

Qwen supplies a detailed synthesis with 68 distinct case references, accurate repeat-member counts and correct alert and feedback reconciliations. It explicitly tests the vendor's supported claims alongside the contradicted claims. Its proposed material correction follows section 6 notice and CVAS approval, with a conditional-renewal and continuity fallback. This is the clearest implementation path among these submitted reports. [Q, sections 2-9]

Its opening nevertheless says the service works for most members most of the time. That is a population conclusion this sample cannot establish. It also describes failures as all occurring under CVC-1.5, despite identifying C004 and C051 under CVC-1.4. Its estimate that notice/concern cases represent roughly no more than 1% of the population is unsupported because the total population of concern cases is not supplied. Later sampling caveats do not remove these claims. [Q, lines 12, 70, 87 and 162; C]

The record-link work also contains errors: seven forwarded items are reported instead of six; two closed items are labeled forwarded; C016's queue item is sent_options, not forwarded. An already-booked contact for M-011 is assumed where only appointment options are established. The report also claims the switch timestamp appeared in the packet and that later self-report makes C005's service outcome visible. Both exceed the supplied evidence. [Q, lines 31, 51, 187, 195-196 and 220; C; E]

### What the differences imply

Luna's rerun needs targeted corrections to contractual reasoning, member-level sampling limits and future change control. Haiku needs a broader audit of case ratings, summary consistency and claims drawn from incomplete operational records. Qwen needs its executive claims reconciled with its careful detailed sections, plus corrections to operational links and unsupported conclusions. Each report offers useful analysis; each still needs review before being relied on as a final assessment.

Renewal preference alone does not distinguish quality. Conditional renewal and deferral pending verification are both defensible. Qwen's categorical claim that non-renewal is unsupported goes beyond choosing a preferred option. Haiku's deferral is not a failure merely because the other reports favor a conditional path. The relevant question is whether the evidence and implementation conditions support the chosen decision. [K; Q, line 206]

## Scope and source register

These findings describe one principal submitted output per model label. The archive includes an additional Luna attempt that informed calibration. The comparison does not isolate the effects of model settings, run-to-run variability, prompting history, access to feedback, cost or elapsed time. A single output per model is insufficient to establish a stable model ranking. Source equivalence is established; equivalent execution conditions have not been independently audited.

The common contract omissions are the clearest shared weakness in this trial set. They support further testing of incorporation, notice recipients and the distinction between noncompliance and incomplete evidence. They do not establish that any model always fails those tasks. The fixed key should remain the evaluation baseline for this set; subsequent substantive revisions would require consistent reassessment.
