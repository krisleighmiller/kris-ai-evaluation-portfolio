# AI Conversation Review Limitations

- The study contains one primary report per model route. It describes these outputs, not the model families in general.
- The comparison was intentionally limited to lower-tier options. It does not estimate how a frontier model would perform.
- The sample contains all six recorded alert cases and four member-or-family-concern cases, plus 90 routine cases. It is enriched and is not a random sample of the reported 1,367-session pilot.
- The 100 conversations represent 72 synthetic members, so repeated observations are not independent.
- One transcript is truncated, two contain encoding artifacts, several operational records are summaries, and referenced attachments are absent.
- Feedback is self-selected: 71 responses exist, 24 have no session ID, and three linked responses point outside the 100-conversation sample.
- The answer key is qualitative. No weights or grade boundaries were agreed before evaluation, so percentages and letter grades are not comparable measures.
- GLM made useful corrections but also missed overclaims and introduced at least one factual error. It is evidence for an assisted review workflow, not a validated sole grader.
- Provider routing, model revisions, OpenCode versions, tool policies, reasoning settings, and run-to-run variation can affect a reproduction.
- The dataset is synthetic. Findings demonstrate review and evidence-checking methods; they are not clinical conclusions about real people or a real service.
