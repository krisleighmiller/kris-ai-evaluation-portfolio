# AI Evaluation Services

You bring the workflow and the question. I design a bounded evaluation to investigate how selected AI models handle it—through a comparative study, a reusable benchmark, a controlled intervention test, or a combination that fits the question.

The purpose is to produce evidence for a specific decision: what worked under the tested conditions, what failed, what still required human verification, and what the experiment did not establish. An engagement begins only after we agree in writing on the question, evidence standard, deliverables, and exclusions.

## What you must supply

The starting point is a sanitized business procedure or workflow. You do not need to design the evaluation or arrive with a finished benchmark, but you must provide enough information to make the task and its acceptance conditions testable:

- What starts the workflow and what information is available at that point.
- What output, decision, or action the workflow requires.
- The rules, exceptions, permissions, and escalation requirements that apply.
- What makes a result acceptable, unacceptable, or uncertain.
- Representative fictional or sanitized examples, including difficult and exceptional cases.
- An available subject-matter expert when correctness depends on domain knowledge that is not fully captured in the written materials.

Do not send personal data or confidential information. Sanitization must preserve the rules, ambiguity, missing information, and exceptions that make the proposed evaluation meaningful. If those conditions cannot be represented safely, the question may need to be narrowed or the engagement may not be a suitable fit.

## What an engagement can investigate

Depending on the agreed scope, an evaluation can investigate questions such as:

- How do selected models perform on this task?
- What mistakes do they make, and how consequential are those mistakes?
- Which outputs or decisions still require human checking?
- Do specified prompt, context, or input changes improve the observed results?
- What tradeoffs appear among accuracy, coverage, uncertainty, cost, and elapsed time?
- Can the evaluation be turned into a repeatable benchmark for future trials?

Other questions can be considered through written scoping. The final question must be narrow enough that the available evidence can support a useful answer. Offline testing can investigate performance on a defined task; by itself, it cannot establish profitability, production safety, or suitability for every real-world condition.

## How domain ambiguities are resolved

I provide evaluation expertise, not automatic expertise in your industry. Domain-specific correctness is established from authoritative written requirements and your designated subject-matter expert.

Ambiguities, conflicting rules, and missing acceptance criteria are documented as written questions. Your expert resolves the domain question, and the resulting decision is recorded before it is used in test design or scoring. When an ambiguity cannot be resolved, I will identify it as a limitation, narrow the affected claim, exclude that condition from scoring, or recommend that the engagement not proceed. An AI model's answer is not treated as authority for resolving your business rules.

## What the written deliverable includes

Each evaluation includes a written findings report and supporting test records. Exact contents are set by the agreed scope.

The findings report explains:

- What was tested: the workflow, evaluation questions, selected models, run conditions, and acceptance criteria.
- What happened: observed performance by meaningful requirement or task category, not only an overall score.
- What failed: consequential examples, effects, and recurring patterns where the evidence supports them.
- What required human verification: outputs or decisions that needed correction, escalation, or source checking.
- What the trials cost: recorded usage, cost, and elapsed-time information where available, with relevant tradeoffs.
- What remains uncertain: limitations, untested conditions, supported conclusions, and warranted next steps.

Supporting test records make those findings traceable to the evaluation. They may include test cases, criteria, submitted prompts, relevant run settings, model outputs, scores, review findings, and available usage records. They are a curated evidence package, not a dump of private working notes or exploratory conversations.

When a reusable benchmark is commissioned, the handoff also includes the agreed test inputs, automated checks or scoring instructions, baseline results, known limitations, and instructions for running future trials. Delivery of a benchmark does not include ongoing maintenance or compatibility guarantees for future models and software versions.

## Written-only communication and response windows

All consultations and project communication take place through email or another agreed written channel. Phone calls and live meetings are not part of the service. Written communication keeps requirements, questions, decisions, approvals, and scope changes documented throughout the evaluation.

Before work begins, the written scope will specify the communication channel, expected response window for each side, review periods, and how schedule changes will be handled when an answer is needed before work can continue. These agreed windows provide predictable communication without implying live or continuous availability.

## What is outside the service

I design evaluations, conduct controlled tests, and report evidence. I do not take responsibility for implementing or operating the workflow being evaluated.

The service does not include:

- Deploying a model or workflow into production.
- Integrating the workflow with your applications, data, accounts, or infrastructure.
- Implementing your production process or making operational decisions on your behalf.
- Ongoing monitoring, incident response, technical support, or model administration.
- Maintaining a benchmark or integration after the agreed handoff.
- Owning production performance, business outcomes, regulatory decisions, or future model behavior.

You retain responsibility for production decisions and for obtaining any implementation, security, legal, compliance, or domain-specialist review the intended use requires. The deliverable is an evidence-backed assessment of defined trials, not a guarantee of production performance.
